# Vision and player experience

## Shared world and different forms of power

Players compete for the Grand Heart in a living world. A fighter can threaten a route, a knowledgeable character can interpret a mechanism, a persuasive trader can secure access, and a thief can change who owns a resource. These strategies share geography, information and an economy.

Classes establish starting training, attributes and learning opportunities. Players then allocate growth, learn skills, improve proficiency, prepare a loadout and potentially specialize. A class is not a fixed final kit. Profession describes economic or social activity and need not match starting class.

Peaceful residents provide ordinary routines and crowd cover. Autonomous contenders can use the same character rules as players. An outfit may suggest an occupation without revealing every learned ability. Dangerous skills still need clear commitments and responses.

## Match experience

The full target is 60–90 minutes, with earlier success possible. Establishment creates resources and information; competition contests valuable opportunities; convergence brings players close to acquisition through their chosen strategies. These are soft pacing aims, not gates that prohibit early progress.

Quiet play needs decisions about work, books, preparation, information and travel. A long match must not become an hour of repetitive chores followed by one fight. Setbacks need recovery and a plausible change of plan.

## Principles

Attributes support combat, exploration and society. A peaceful build need not match a fighter's duel strength to be competitive. Geography, surveillance, services and preparation can protect it, while remaining contestable.

Knowledge, proficiency and equipped skills are separate. Every learned active uses the same slot pool, including contextual utility. A utility menu is not a free extra hotbar.

Medium lethality and meaningful assassination counterplay remain goals. Low attributes must not make basic controls unusable, and additional scaling must not remove readable warnings.

World progress must have visible causes and legal actions, including AI actions. Towers are infrastructure as well as conquest prerequisites. Their loss changes information and incentives.

Success requires human evidence of understandable build choices, alternate strategies, useful information and recovery. Code completeness or a bot victory does not establish enjoyment or balance.
