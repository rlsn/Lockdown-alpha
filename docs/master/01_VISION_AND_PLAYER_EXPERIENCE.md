# Vision and player experience

## The game we are making

**Direction.** We are making an online multiplayer game with the accessibility of an io game, crowd stealth, action combat and growth within a match. Everyone controlling a contender can be dangerous, but constant killing must not be the only sensible behavior. The city should contain people doing ordinary work as well as actors pursuing power and the central prize.

The Grand Heart is the shared victory objective. Players can approach it through different strategies. They may dismantle its defensive network and claim it in a showdown, or complete a demanding peaceful progression that provides another way to obtain it while those defences remain. These routes compete for the same unique outcome.

Death creates a setback rather than immediate permanent elimination. Time lost while respawning, gold loss and recoverable objective possessions create stakes. The penalty grows with progression and match pressure, so a late death can leave a contender unable to return before someone claims the Heart.

The full match target remains 20–30 minutes. Solo competition and four-player groups remain intended modes. Population, team counts, commercial model and final presentation are undecided. A release can test a shorter free for all without changing the full game target.

## How a match should feel

The opening should offer understandable routines. Public surveillance supports commerce, civilian movement and relatively safe growth. A player can observe patterns, complete work, scout routes, place a ward or risk a wild area. Hiding among a credible population is useful even when no attack is planned.

The middle of a match should produce conflicting goals. Towers, camps, rare opportunities and profession milestones offer different rewards. Taking down surveillance can open the aggressive route while making travel and other objectives more dangerous. Peaceful competitors still have an active plan rather than waiting for combat players to determine the ending.

The closing period should make commitment costly. Death penalties are long enough that defending a route, stealing an important component or interrupting a Heart claim matters. The outcome follows an objective action. Being the last temporarily living contender is an opportunity to claim the Heart, not an automatic victory.

## Design principles

### Ordinary activity must be credible

Civilian routines need destinations and pauses that players can imitate. A city where every NPC seeks a fight provides little cover. The peaceful population must remain substantial and recover after losses. Autonomous competitors may pursue the same opportunities as players without turning every public street into continuous combat.

### Safety must vary across the map

Public surveillance offers shared information, not invulnerability. Wild areas offer fewer observers and more dangerous opportunities. Terrain, trees and elevation create routes where a player can disappear or scout an opponent. An active tower should materially improve awareness; its loss should be noticeable in what players can see.

### Peaceful growth must have a competitive purpose

Profession activity needs useful XP, gold or materials and a real route toward victory. A peaceful route can involve exposure, travel and contestable milestones without requiring a player kill. It must not be merely a slow way to buy the same mandatory combat sequence.

### Assassination must retain a response

Medium lethality, positional preparation and victim counterplay remain the combat direction. Fog and higher ground should create preparation advantages without silently removing a required warning. Adding stats or artifacts must not accidentally turn ordinary contact into an unavoidable kill.

### The city must progress through visible causes

AI contenders, population routines and scheduled world activity should keep a match moving without requiring a human to trigger everything. Important changes must have an inspectable cause. Do not damage towers or grant an AI victory through an invisible script presented as simulated player activity.

### Every winning route must remain contestable

A peaceful contender needs a credible way to win while towers remain. Other players need a reasonable chance to observe, interrupt or redirect that attempt. A global announcement can reveal objective activity without exposing every hidden actor or private inventory.

## What success would mean

Players should be able to describe why they chose a public or wild route, when they preserved concealment, how their growth supported a Heart strategy and what a death cost them. They should recognize at least one alternative to their chosen route. A satisfying result requires understandable objective ownership and server resolved outcomes.

These are design hypotheses. Neither the initial concept conversations nor the specification packages establish fun, retention, market demand or balance. Those claims require observations from actual play.
