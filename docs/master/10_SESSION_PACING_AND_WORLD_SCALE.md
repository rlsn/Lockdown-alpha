# Match flow, pacing and world scale

## The core loop

Observe a local opportunity → choose a plan → earn or acquire resources → learn and prepare → travel/expose that investment → resolve an encounter → reassess → pursue access → contest the Heart. Recovery feeds back into that loop. It should not force every player through the same chore sequence.

Three interlocking loops operate at different timescales. Moment-to-moment play is movement, observation, targeting and response. Development is work, exploration, acquisition, training and preparation. Strategy is choosing a route, controlling exposure, reacting to public events and staging a final attempt. Each small action should have a visible relationship to a larger purpose.

## Full-session target

The intended match lasts 60–90 minutes, with a 90-minute default deadline and 60/75-minute validation variants. Legal earlier wins remain possible. The target describes desired pacing, not an artificial wait before victory unlocks.

| Observation band | Expected emphasis | Questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Establishment, roughly 0–20 min | Local orientation, first income and toolkit | Can players identify a useful next action? |
| Competition, roughly 20–45 min | Rare sources, opponents and investment choices | Do plans differ, intersect and change? |
| Route commitment, roughly 45–70 min | Towers, endorsements, forge, keys and transport | Are costs/exposure meaningful without repetitive grind? |
| Convergence, roughly 70–90 min | Forward staging, warnings and claim attempts | Can nearby rivals respond and understand the outcome? |

These are hypotheses. Cities, sources and routes are available from match start. If normal games repeatedly end at 10–20 minutes or wander to NoAcquisition, record that failure and revise explicit placement/progression/tuning. Do not hide it behind a minimum-win rule.

## Scale and useful travel

The 2.4 km world supports distinct regional commitments. At baseline 420 cm/s, a 1 km walk is about four minutes before detours. Travel should include information, approach and exposure decisions. It becomes dead time when routes are empty, safe and repetitive.

Measure first rival sighting, journey/idle share, longest empty segment, source contest frequency, useful stops and reasons for rerouting. Compare native paths, graph estimates and actual walked times. Move existing content or shorten links before increasing speed or population as a blanket fix.

Warnings need plausible responses from local staging, not guaranteed responses from every city. The nearby antechamber resolves activation-to-Heart feasibility; macro positioning still matters.

## Progression and recovery pacing

Extra duration does not multiply every reward. Full-profile camp timings, work quotas, stocks and endowments are explicit. Prices, natural gates, slot limits and route costs remain meaningful. Record normal level/gold distributions and counter affordability rather than assuming every contender reaches level 15.

Repeated work should create route exposure and choices, not compulsory monotonous labor. Death should cost enough to affect a plan but leave a useful recovery path. Observe whether late downtime produces tension or disengagement.

## Evidence boundaries

The retained v08_integration profile is a 35-minute, 1–4-contender short-map integration test. It is not evidence for full-session balance. A long automated soak establishes stability in its measured workload. Full pacing needs normal legal progression and human observation, including at least one 60+ minute human-involved match under v0.9 acceptance.

Record winner/route/time, failed openings, travel/idle intervals, progression, deaths/recovery, local density and participants' explanations. Separate normal matches from stress fixtures that suppress final acquisition.
