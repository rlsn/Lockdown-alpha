# Acquisition and build diversity

## Independent progression axes

Equipment tier describes identity, function and scarcity. Standard/Fine/Masterwork describes modest eligible workmanship. Spell Circle describes complexity/gates; trained rank describes mastery; a future named variant would describe a tactical alternative. These axes must not collapse into a single larger-is-always-better ladder.

Current native v0.8 coverage is 52 catalog abilities, 80 items, 20 recipes and nine presets. These counts describe available implemented definitions, not proof that every combination is balanced or discoverable. Preserve stable IDs/catalogs while adding world sources.

| Source | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Starts and trainers | Legible initial direction and reliable knowledge investment |
| Official counters | Paid common tools/counters; shared rare-stock constraints |
| Regional markets and peers | Scarcity, convenience, transport margins and specialization |
| Assembly/work orders | Predictable incremental builds from owned or paid inputs |
| Specialist stations | Quality and advanced transforms at real geography |
| Ruins/camps/boss/towers | Finite risk, keys, catalysts, books and relics |
| Theft/death recovery | Change ownership of existing goods |

## Branching decisions

Recipes should preserve useful component choices and create understandable branches. A range staff, dispelling staff and repositioning staff answer different situations. Upgrading may sacrifice an offhand, quality, flexibility or a scarce input. Strong gear compresses functions without erasing every weakness.

Ordinary chains remain short. Assembly uses exact unbound/unreserved inputs, capacity checks, fees, Standard output and lineage. Specialized quality improves only eligible numbers. It does not improve natural gates, hard-control durations, charges or every property at once.

Full work orders can purchase missing immediate official inputs at the proper station. They cannot recursively manufacture a missing scarce component, create a Heartbreaker shortcut or pay the player assembly XP.

## Accessible answers and scarcity

Basic counter-items remain available through reliable paid supply, so one buyer cannot corner all dispel/detection. Advanced books have shared finite stock. Unique world sources supply scarce keys/catalysts/Fragment/capstones; similar-looking containers do not create extra copies.

A player may buy, trade, steal or recover a rare component instead of defeating its source personally. Heartbreaker should be economically accessible across classes while retaining vulnerable material/forge investment.

## Why one build cannot do everything

Six shared actions, four accessory activations, eleven physical bindings, one capstone, natural investment, MP/cooldowns and carrying capacity constrain coverage. A larger bag supports logistics, not immediate access. Quiet/station preparation creates an opportunity to anticipate an encounter rather than swap every answer during it.

Class traits are narrow immutable starting identity. Signature suggestions still consume shared slots and ordinary knowledge remains cross-class. No hidden exclusive class kit emerges from a tooltip or starter adapter.

## Evaluating diversity

Test whether different builds offer distinct plans, whether cheap answers remain useful late, and whether players can explain an upgrade's sacrifice. Record actual purchases, preparation changes, unused skills, common pairings and route outcomes.

Catalog quantity is not diversity. If players repeatedly choose one generalist kit, inspect slot compression, opportunity cost, affordability and counter access before adding more items. Spell variants, subclasses and random affixes remain proposals unless selected with explicit budgets.
